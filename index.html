Linear Frequency Modulation (LFM)

Instantaneous Frequency f(t) vs. Accumulated Phase φ(t)

In an FMCW radar chirp, signal amplitude remains constant while instantaneous frequency sweeps linearly with slope S = B / T_c. Because phase is the time-integral of frequency, a linear frequency ramp generates a quadratic phase progression φ(t) = 2π(f_c·t + ½S·t²), mapping time delay directly into frequency shift.

1. FMCW Range & De-Chirp Equations

Chirp Slope: S = B / T_c [Hz/s]
Round-Trip Delay: τ = 2R / c [s]
Range Beat Freq: f_r = S·τ = (2·B·R) / (c·T_c)
Target Range: R = (c · T_c · f_r) / (2 · B)

Mixer multiplication converts high-frequency RF time delay into a low-frequency sinusoidal beat tone proportional to R.

2. Spectral Resolution & Windowing

Rayleigh FFT Bin Width: Δf_min = 1 / T_c [Hz]
Range Resolution: ΔR = c / (2 · B) [m]
Sawtooth Coupling Error: δR = -(f_c·T_c / B)·v
Hann Window Sidelobe: -31.5 dB (vs -13.3 dB Rect)

Range resolution depends exclusively on sweep bandwidth B. Windowing suppresses spectral leakage sidelobes at the cost of a slightly wider main lobe.

3. Doppler & Triangular R-v Decoupling

Doppler Frequency: f_d = (2 · v · f_c) / c = 2v / λ
Up-Chirp Beat: f_b,up = |f_r - f_d|
Down-Chirp Beat: f_b,down = |f_r + f_d|
Exact Decoupling: f_r = ½(f_up + f_dn), f_d = ½(f_dn - f_up)

Opposite sweep slopes (+S and -S) invert the sign of range frequency relative to Doppler shift, enabling exact algebraic separation.
